# Common garden pests

## Aphids
Small green, black or white insects clustered on new shoots and under leaves; leaves become sticky (honeydew) and may curl. Ants often farm them. Treatment: knock them off with a strong spray of water, or spray mild soapy water (about 1 teaspoon of plain liquid soap per litre of water) on both sides of the leaves, then repeat every 2–3 days for a week. Test the spray on a few leaves first and do not spray in hot midday sun. Ladybirds and lacewings eat aphids, so avoid broad insecticides.

## Slugs and snails
Ragged holes in leaves and silvery slime trails, worst after rain and at night. Lettuce, basil and seedlings are favourites. Treatment: hand-pick them at night with a torch, put copper tape around pots, set beer traps (a shallow dish of beer sunk into the soil), and remove hiding places such as boards and dense weeds near the bed.

## Spider mites
Tiny pale speckles (stippling) on leaves and fine webbing under the leaves, worst in hot, dry conditions and indoors in heated rooms. Treatment: rinse the undersides of the leaves with water, raise humidity, isolate the plant, and repeat every few days. Insecticidal soap also works.

## Fungus gnats
Small black flies around pots, with larvae that live in constantly wet soil. They are a sign of overwatering. Treatment: let the top 2–3 cm of soil dry out between waterings, water from below, and use yellow sticky traps to catch the adults.

## Flea beetles, caterpillars and whiteflies
Flea beetles leave many tiny shot holes in arugula, radish and kale leaves; cover the crop with fine fleece and keep the soil moist. Caterpillars (for example cabbage worms on kale) are best hand-picked; check under leaves for eggs. Whiteflies fly up in a cloud when the plant is touched; use yellow sticky traps and insecticidal soap on the leaf undersides.

## When to ask for help
If a pest keeps coming back after two weeks of treatment, or you cannot identify it, take a clear photo of the insect and the damage and ask your local agricultural extension office or a garden centre.
