# Plant diseases and disorders

## Powdery mildew
A white, flour-like powder on the upper side of leaves, common on cucumbers, squash, peas and mint in humid weather with poor airflow. Treatment: remove the worst leaves, water at the base instead of over the leaves, give plants more space and airflow, and spray a mix of 1 part milk to 9 parts water once a week. Do not compost badly infected leaves.

## Damping off
Seedlings suddenly collapse at the soil line, the stem looks thin and pinched, and white fuzz may appear on the soil. It is caused by soil fungi in wet, cold, stagnant conditions. Prevention: sow in fresh seed-starting mix, do not overwater, water from below, give good airflow and light, and thin crowded seedlings. Collapsed seedlings cannot be saved; resow in clean mix.

## Root rot
Caused by roots sitting in waterlogged soil. The plant wilts even though the soil is wet, lower leaves turn yellow, and stems go dark and mushy at the base. Fix: improve drainage, let the soil dry, and repot into fresh mix after cutting away brown, mushy roots. Severe root rot is usually fatal.

## Tomato leaf spot and blight
Brown spots with yellow halos that start on the lowest tomato leaves and move upward. Remove affected leaves, keep leaves dry by watering at the base, mulch the soil so rain does not splash spores up, and prune the lowest leaves so they do not touch the soil. Fast-spreading dark patches on leaves and stems in cool wet weather can be late blight; remove and bin the plant to protect neighbours.

## Blossom end rot
A dark, sunken, leathery patch on the bottom of tomato or pepper fruit. It is not a disease: the fruit could not get enough calcium, almost always because watering was uneven. Fix: water deeply and regularly so the soil stays evenly moist, mulch, and remove affected fruit. Later fruit usually grows normally.

## Bolting
Lettuce, spinach, cilantro and arugula send up a tall flower stalk and the leaves turn bitter. It is triggered by heat and long days. Grow these crops in spring and autumn, give afternoon shade, keep them watered, and sow small batches every 2–3 weeks. Once a plant bolts, harvest what you can; it will not go back.
