# Frost and cold protection

## Which plants are frost-tender
Frost-tender plants are damaged or killed when the temperature drops to about 0–2 °C: basil, tomatoes, peppers and chilies, beans, cucumbers, squash and microgreens. Basil is even more sensitive and gets black spots below about 10 °C. Frost-hardy crops survive light frost and often taste sweeter after it: kale, spinach, lettuce (light frost only), arugula, radish, carrots, green onions, peas and mint. Greenhand sends a frost alert when the forecast low is at or below 2 °C within the next 48 hours.

## How to protect plants on a frost night
1. Water the soil in the afternoon before the frost; moist soil holds more heat than dry soil.
2. At sunset, cover plants with frost cloth (horticultural fleece), an old bed sheet or a cardboard box. Let the cover reach the ground so it traps the soil's warmth. Do not let plastic touch the leaves, because plastic transfers cold.
3. Move pots indoors, into a garage, or against a house wall where it is a few degrees warmer.
4. Remove the covers the next morning once the temperature is above freezing so the plants get light and do not overheat.

## After a frost
Wait a day before pruning damaged growth: leaves that look wilted in the morning sometimes recover. Black, translucent or mushy leaves are dead and can be cut off. If a frost-tender plant is badly damaged late in the season, harvest everything that is usable, including green tomatoes, which ripen indoors on a windowsill or in a paper bag.

## End of the season
In cold climates (for example hardiness zone 5b, where the first frost usually arrives in October), frost-tender annuals such as tomatoes and basil finish their season with the first hard frost. Bring potted herbs like basil indoors to a sunny window before nights fall below 10 °C. Hardy greens such as kale and spinach can keep growing into early winter, especially under a cover.
