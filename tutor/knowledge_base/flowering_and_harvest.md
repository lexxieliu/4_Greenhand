# Flowering, pollination and harvest

## Helping tomatoes and peppers set fruit
Tomatoes, peppers and beans are self-pollinating, but on a balcony with few insects they set more fruit if you gently shake the flower trusses or tap the stems each morning while they are flowering. Flowers may drop without setting fruit when nights are below about 13 °C, days are above about 32 °C, watering is uneven, or the plant got too much nitrogen. Cucumbers and squash need insects or hand pollination with a small brush.

## When to harvest tomatoes
Cherry tomatoes are usually ready 7–8 weeks after the plant starts flowering in warm weather; cool autumn weather slows ripening. Pick when the fruit has its full colour (red, or deep golden-orange for 'Sungold') and comes off with a gentle tug. Before the first frost, pick green tomatoes that have reached full size and ripen them indoors.

## Harvesting herbs
Harvest herbs often: it keeps them bushy. Cut basil and mint just above a pair of leaves (a leaf node), where two new shoots will grow. Never remove more than one third of the plant at once. Pinch off basil flower buds to keep the leaves tender. Cilantro bolts quickly in heat, so harvest the outer leaves young.

## Harvesting leafy greens
Use the cut-and-come-again method for lettuce, spinach, kale and arugula: pick the outer leaves once they are about 8–10 cm long and leave the centre to keep growing. Harvest in the morning when leaves are crisp. Kale leaves taste sweeter after a light frost.

## Root crops and peas
Radishes are ready 25–30 days after sowing, when the root shoulders are about 2–3 cm wide; left too long they turn woody and hot. Carrots are ready when the top of the root is about 2 cm across, usually 60–80 days after sowing. Pick snap peas and bush beans every 2–3 days while they are young and tender; regular picking makes the plant produce more.
