# Seeds and seedlings

## Sowing seeds
Sow seeds about two to three times as deep as the seed is wide: tiny seeds such as lettuce and basil are pressed onto the surface or barely covered, while beans and peas go about 2–3 cm deep. Keep the soil evenly moist until they sprout; a cover or plastic lid helps, but remove it once seedlings appear. Most vegetable seeds germinate best at 18–24 °C.

## Thinning
When seedlings have their first true leaves, thin them to the spacing on the seed packet so each plant has room. Snip the extra seedlings at soil level with scissors instead of pulling them, which would disturb the roots of the ones you keep. Thinnings of lettuce, radish and kale are edible as microgreens.

## Hardening off
Seedlings raised indoors must get used to outdoor sun, wind and cold before planting out. Over 7–10 days, put them outside in a sheltered spot for a few hours, increasing the time and sun exposure each day, and bring them in at night at first. Skipping this step causes sunburn, wilting and shock.

## Transplanting
Transplant on a cloudy day or in the evening. Water the seedling an hour before, dig a hole as deep as the root ball (tomatoes can be planted deeper, up to the first leaves, because they grow roots along the buried stem), firm the soil gently and water well. Wait until after your last frost date to transplant frost-tender plants outdoors.

## Seeds that do not sprout
Common causes: the soil dried out, the seeds were planted too deep, the soil was too cold, or the seeds were old. Check the germination time for your crop before deciding they failed, keep the soil moist and warm, and resow if nothing appears after twice the expected germination time.
