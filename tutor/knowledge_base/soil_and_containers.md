# Soil and containers

## Potting mix versus garden soil
Use bagged potting mix (container mix) in pots, never garden soil: garden soil compacts in a pot, drains badly and can carry pests and diseases. For raised beds, mix good topsoil with compost. Most vegetables and herbs grow best in slightly acidic to neutral soil, pH 6.0–7.0.

## Drainage
Every container needs drainage holes. A layer of gravel at the bottom does not improve drainage and can make waterlogging worse. Raise pots on feet or bricks so water can escape, and empty saucers after watering.

## Choosing a pot size
Bigger pots dry out more slowly and are more forgiving. Rough minimum sizes: herbs such as basil, mint and cilantro 15–20 cm wide; lettuce, spinach and arugula 15 cm deep; radishes 15 cm deep; carrots 25–30 cm deep; bush beans and peas 20 cm deep; peppers and chilies 10 litres; cherry tomatoes at least 20 litres per plant; strawberries 15 cm deep or a hanging basket. Mint spreads aggressively, so always give it its own pot.

## Mulch
A 3–5 cm layer of mulch (straw, shredded leaves or compost) on the soil surface keeps moisture in, keeps roots cool, and stops rain splashing soil and disease spores onto leaves. Keep mulch a few centimetres away from the stems.

## Repotting
Repot when roots circle the bottom of the pot or grow out of the drainage holes, or when the plant dries out within a day of watering. Move up one pot size, water the plant an hour before, loosen circling roots gently, and keep it out of strong sun for a couple of days afterwards.
