# Light and indoor growing

## How much sun plants need
Full sun means at least 6–8 hours of direct sun a day: tomatoes, peppers, beans, basil and strawberries need this to fruit well. Partial sun means 3–6 hours: lettuce, spinach, kale, cilantro, mint and microgreens are happy here, and in hot weather they prefer afternoon shade. Watch your balcony or yard for a day and count the hours of direct sun before choosing plants.

## Signs of too little light
Leggy plants with long, thin, pale stems that lean towards the window, small leaves, and few or no flowers. Fix: move the plant to the brightest spot (a south-facing window in the northern hemisphere), rotate the pot a quarter turn every few days so it grows evenly, pinch leggy tips, or add a grow light.

## Grow lights
LED grow lights let you grow herbs, leafy greens and microgreens indoors all year. Keep the light about 15–30 cm above the plants and run it for 12–16 hours a day on a timer. Seedlings under too little light stretch and flop over.

## Indoor care in the heating season
Heated indoor air is dry, which stresses plants and invites spider mites. Group plants together, stand pots on a tray of moist pebbles, keep them away from radiators and cold window glass at night, and check the soil more often. Indoor plants are not affected by rain or frost alerts.

## Sunburn and heat
Leaves that suddenly turn white or papery after a move into strong sun are sunburned. Introduce plants to full sun gradually. During heat waves above 32 °C, water early in the morning, move pots into afternoon shade, and use shade cloth over beds.
