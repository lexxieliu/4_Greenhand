# Safety for edible gardens

## Toxic parts of common food plants
Not every part of a food plant is edible. Tomato, potato, pepper and eggplant leaves and stems contain toxic compounds and should not be eaten; green potato skin is also toxic. Rhubarb leaves are poisonous. Never eat wild plants, berries or mushrooms unless an expert has positively identified them, and keep plants that are toxic to pets out of their reach.

## Using sprays and pesticides safely
Start with the gentlest option: hand-picking, water sprays and mild soapy water. If you use any pesticide, fungicide or fertilizer concentrate, follow the product label exactly, including the waiting time before harvest, wear gloves, and keep products in their original containers, away from children and pets. Never mix different chemical products together. Do not spray in hot midday sun or when bees are active on flowers.

## Washing and eating your harvest
Wash produce under running water before eating, especially leafy greens grown near the ground. Wash your hands after handling soil, compost or manure.

## Emergencies and expert help
If a child or pet may have eaten a toxic plant or garden chemical, contact a doctor, veterinarian or poison control right away. For serious or spreading plant problems you cannot identify, your local agricultural extension office gives free, local advice. AI-generated advice can be wrong, so verify anything safety-related with a reliable source.
